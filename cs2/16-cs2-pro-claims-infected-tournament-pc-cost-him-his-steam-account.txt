A major security incident has overshadowed the $1 million XSE Pro League LAN in Guangzhou, China, after professional CS2 players claimed their practice PCs had been loaded with malicious software.

BetBoom’s Kirill “Magnojez” Rodnov posted a video of an antivirus scan flagging multiple spyware and trojan files on his own setup.

His in-game leader Aleksandr “zorte” Zagodyrenko posted on Telegram describing his situation, revealing his tournament PC contained trojans alongside a torrent client and a private World of Warcraft server client.

The situation arose because BIG’s (a German organization) tournament SSDs reportedly stopped working, forcing players onto compromised practice machines instead.

According to Dust2.in, Magnojez believes logging into the infected Chinese PCs is what caused him to lose access to his Steam account entirely.

The specific software identified was a remote access trojan capable of extracting saved passwords, stealing session tokens to bypass two-factor authentication and accessing cryptocurrency wallets.

The security incident is just one part of a wider organizational collapse at the event, which has been widely described as potentially one of the worst CS2 tournaments in history.

Opening matches were delayed by nearly two hours, with the backlog pushing some games back by five to six hours. Some PCs reportedly did not even have CS2 installed.

The broadcast suffered from broken audio and incorrect player names and pictures, and a transformer blew up outside the venue mid-match between 9z and SINNERS, cutting power to the building for around an hour.

Spotted on Chinese social media by a Reddit user, the majority of tournament officials at the event were university student volunteers with no previous LAN experience.
